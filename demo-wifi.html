🔍 Here's what just happened — and why it's a trap

The moment you clicked "Connect," this page threw away whatever you typed — it was never read, stored, or sent anywhere. A real attacker's page would do the opposite: quietly capture it. Here's what should have tipped you off before you typed anything:

  • 🚩 No wifi network needs your account password. Real wifi logins never ask for the password you use on email, school portals, or social media. The instant a "connect to wifi" screen asks for that, it's not about wifi.
  • 🚩 Artificial urgency. The countdown ("offer ends in 4:59!") is designed to make you rush and skip thinking.
  • 🚩 Too-good-to-be-true free offer. "Free" is the bait; the real goal is the credentials you type.
  • 🚩 Unverifiable network identity. Anyone can name a wifi hotspot "Student-Free-WiFi" — a name alone proves nothing about who runs it.
  • 🚩 Re-using a real password anywhere outside the real service is the actual danger — even typing it into a fake form "just to see" can train muscle memory that makes you faster to fall for the real thing.

The rule: wifi portals should only ever ask for things like a room number, a voucher code, or an email you don't mind sharing — never a password you use elsewhere.
